# Switching to Metric System

I finally pulled the plug and abandoned the archaic system of weights and measures of the good ol' United States of America. Actually, this was a product of [visiting New Zealand](./2026-09-20-fiji-nz.md) over a month ago, and I'm finally just writing about it now.

## Time

Actually, I've had my clocks set in a 24-hour format long before I went to New Zealand. I still find myself converting in my headwhat 17:00 is, for example, but slowly it's becoming more and more second nature.

As for [dates](./2023-10-04-dates.md), I personally like the `YYYY-MM-DD` format the best. It makes the most sense when paired with `HH:MM:SS`, because you go from "largest" timescale to "smallest." Also, if you name files this way, it will naturally sort by date. This is one frustration about non-US dates. They got everything else right, but why, *why*, ***why*** `DD-MM-YYYY`? At least it's not the `MM/DD/YYYY` monstrosity adopted by the US.

## Temperature

This was the next to go. New Zealand, like the rest of the world, uses degrees Celsius for temperature and weather reporting. It is a much more scientifically-defined system. 0C is the freezing point of water and 100C is the boiling point. I was curious about the Fahrenheight scale, so this is what I got from a Wikipedia article, paraphrased:

> The zero point was determined by placing a thermometer in a mixture of brine: ammonium, ice, and water in a 1:1:1 ratio.
> Human body temperature was defined as 96 degrees.

Makes total sense. Also, as we know, human body temperature is not 96F anyway.

Going back to the story, on the trip, I finally converted my weather app over to Celsius. It was shocking to see temperatures originally in the 50s-60s (F) immediately drop to 10s-20s (C).

## Distance

This was my latest addition. In Strava, I always counted my distance in miles and speed in minutes per mile. I finally abandoned that for kilometers and minutes per kilometer. This one felt good as all my distances dramatically increased from the 10s-20s (mi) to 20s-30s (km). However, this will be tricky because I won't want to slowly drift back down as I want to keep my weekly distances up.

To adjust for this mental trickery, I need to make sure that my new monthly goal (originally 60mi per month) is about 100km per month.

I left Google Maps alone in terms of miles. I also cannot change my car's speedometer to km/hr, but even if I could, that would be a very bad idea as all road signs in the United States are in miles per hour (mph).
